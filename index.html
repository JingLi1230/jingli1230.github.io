Jing Li

Dr. Jing Li is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Psychology and Counseling at Pittsburg State University. She specializes in psychometrics, applied statistics, and quantitative research methodology. Her research focuses on developing and applying advanced quantitative methods to enhance the validity, fairness, and interpretability of educational and psychological assessments.

Dr. Li's methodological expertise includes Rasch measurement theory (RMT), explanatory item response modeling (EIRM), structural equation modeling (SEM), and diagnostic classification modeling (DCM). She applies these methods to examine item functioning, detect subgroup differences, and evaluate the quality of measurement instruments across fields such as education, psychology, and public health.

Her recent work also explores the intersection of artificial intelligence and education, including evaluating large language models (LLMs) as raters, examining fairness in AI-based assessment, and leveraging AI-derived item features to predict item difficulty in educational tests. Dr. Li earned her B.A. in Psychology from Michigan State University, her M.Ed. in Educational Psychology from Miami University, and both her Ph.D. in Educational Psychology and M.S. in Statistics from the University of Georgia.
