Jing Li

Welcome! I am an assistant professor in the Department of Psychology and Counseling at Pittsburg State University. Feel free to explore my website to learn more about my research, teaching, and academic background.

Research Interests

My research focuses on developing and applying advanced quantitative methods to enhance the validity, fairness, and interpretability of educational and psychological assessments. My methodological expertise includes Rasch measurement theory (RMT), explanatory item response modeling (EIRM), structural equation modeling (SEM), and diagnostic classification modeling (DCM). I apply these methods to examine item functioning, detect subgroup differences, and evaluate the quality of measurement instruments across fields such as education, psychology, and public health.

My recent work also explores the intersection of artificial intelligence and education, including evaluating large language models (LLMs) as raters, examining fairness in AI-based assessment, and leveraging AI-derived item features to predict item difficulty in educational tests.
